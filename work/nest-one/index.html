What it is

Nest One is an indoor drone from Nested Robotics, which I co-founded. It is meant to bring small things, like keys or glasses, to people who find stairs and long walks hard. So far the whole project lives in software, CAD and simulation. My part right now is the downwash study.

How it works

To grab an item, the drone hovers about 15 cm above a table or the floor. The air from its rotors spreads out along the surface and can blow a pill packet away before the gripper closes. I looked for the duct and prop that make the least of that wind.

The prop is a toroidal, closed-loop design: each blade leaves the hub and comes back. I sized it with blade-element theory and NeuralFoil. I ran duct shapes through OpenFOAM CFD in WSL, screened 54 designs, then re-ran the finalists time-accurately. After that I ran a full 3D CFD of the prop turning inside its duct, to 1,800 iterations. It showed the prop makes about 8% less thrust and needs 11% more torque than the blade-element design said, so it has to spin at about 22,200 rpm to hover. CalculiX FEA checked the duct and prop for strength, and I re-ran it at the speeds the 3D CFD gave. Scripts build every part as a STEP file.

Where it stands

In simulation, one duct with a 3 degree diffuser and stator vanes cut the floor wind at grab height by 42% against today’s ring. The current printable prop weighs 2.6 g. The first printed design broke at its arm roots in the hover FEA, so this one has level arms. At full throttle it comes within 7% of the nylon’s strength, and hover stress where each loop closes is about twice the long-life limit.

In November I built prototype v3 in Fusion and exported it as STEP: the drone and the nest it docks on to charge. It has four of these ducts, toroidal rotors molded in carbon-filled PEEK, and a claw for picking things up. Nothing has been printed, spun or flown. The next step is a thrust stand and floor-wind test.
