VC2 core regulator

Two-phase buck converter, 12 V to 1.0 V at 20 A.

2026Simulated

vc2-regulator.txtNovember 2026page 001

What it is

VC2 is a two-phase synchronous buck regulator that turns a 12 V bus into 1.0 V at up to 20 A. It is a scaled-down version of the voltage regulator modules that sit around a CPU or GPU die. Those use many more phases at hundreds of amps, but the design problems are the same. At two phases, I can work through all of them by hand and in simulation.

How it works

An LTC3855 controller runs both phases in peak current mode, 180 degrees apart. Each phase has a TI power block, with both MOSFETs in one package, and a 0.56 µH inductor. The controller senses each phase’s current from the inductor’s own winding resistance, so there is no sense resistor in the high-current path. Interleaving the phases cuts the input capacitor RMS current from 5.8 A to 3.9 A.

A Python script computes every component value from the spec. I checked the control loop with an averaged AC model and the cycle-by-cycle behavior with a switching model, both in ngspice.

Where it stands

Requirements, component selection, compensation, both simulations and the BOM are done. The loop crosses over at 46 kHz with 74 degrees of phase margin, and a 5 to 15 A load step stays within about 27 mV. Schematic capture, the 4-layer KiCad layout and a check against the vendor’s LTspice model have not started. The switching model uses ideal switches, so losses come from hand calculations, and one controller gain is estimated from a datasheet graph.
