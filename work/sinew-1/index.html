SINEW-1

Tendon-driven robot arm and hand, driven by a Quest 3.

Oct 2026Designed, not built

sinew-1.txtNovember 2026page 001

What it is

SINEW-1 is a right arm and hand at 1.1 times the size of an adult man’s. I want to drive it from a Meta Quest 3 and feel what it touches through a force-feedback glove. The recorded sessions are meant to become training data for a model that makes humanoid hand movements.

How it works

All 18 hand and wrist “muscles” sit in the forearm and pull tendons through Bowden sheaths. Each muscle is the same module: a gimbal motor, a printed 5:1 planetary, a twin tendon spool and its own controller board. Five GIM actuators drive the shoulder, elbow and forearm rotation. With the two wrist axes, the arm has 7 degrees of freedom.

The Quest’s WebXR hand tracking feeds a retargeter and a 7-DOF IK solver on the PC. The glove renders contact locally at 1 kHz while the PC updates its virtual walls at 500 Hz. In simulation that stayed stable with up to 150 ms of network delay. A recorder logs every stream and exports a LeRobot-style dataset.

Where it stands

Nothing is built. The CAD is complete in Fusion, all twelve boards pass ERC and DRC, and a MuJoCo simulation runs the real firmware control core. In it, the arm reaches, lifts and holds a 300 g bottle. Open problems include a grasp that squeezes far harder than it needs to and hub connectors that can’t be mated inside the forearm spine. The WebXR page has only run in a desktop browser, and no model has been trained yet.
