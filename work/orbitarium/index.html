Orbitarium

A Purdue planner that turns finished assignments into planets.

Oct 2026Runs locally

orbitarium.txtNovember 2026page 001

What it is

Orbitarium is the planner I use for school. It reads my Purdue Brightspace calendar and any published Google or Outlook calendars, collects every deadline in one place, and plans when I should work on each one. To make finishing things feel like something, every completed assignment earns a discovery: a planet, moon, comet or satellite that joins a solar system I can fly around.

How it works

It runs on Next.js with a libSQL database: a local SQLite file on my machine, or Turso when it’s hosted. I give each assignment an estimate, and the planner fits work sessions around classes, meetings, sleep and a buffer before the deadline, then replans anything I miss. Schedules can come in as ICS or CSV files, or as a photo or a sentence that Gemini reads and I review before anything is saved. Calendar links and keys are encrypted at rest, and reminders go out as push notifications 12 hours, 2 hours, 1 hour and 30 minutes before something is due.

The universe is a canvas scene: procedurally drawn planets lit by their sun, moons and satellites that orbit them, and a camera that flies into a planet when you click it. There’s also a command palette, focus timers and an export to .ics.

Where it stands

It runs on my computer and I use it. It’s set up to deploy to Vercel, but the scheduled reminders need a hosted scheduler that I’m still checking before I rely on it. The code isn’t public.
